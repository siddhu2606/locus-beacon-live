LOCUS Beacon

Indoor positioning from real Bluetooth signal strength. One Android phone is the receiver, two or more are beacons. The estimate and its uncertainty appear live on a laptop. It works with no internet.

How it works

  1. Beacon phones broadcast a small Bluetooth Low Energy packet carrying their beacon number (1–6). Place them at known spots: for 3 beacons, a triangle.
  2. The receiver phone scans, measures the signal strength (RSSI) from each beacon and serves the dashboard over its own hotspot or Wi-Fi.
  3. The laptop opens the phone's address, converts RSSI to distance, and runs the LOCUS filter: trust scoring per beacon, motion history, a position with a 90% uncertainty ellipse.

Offline

  • Bluetooth is phone to phone. No internet.
  • The dashboard is bundled inside the APK. The receiver serves it at http://<phone-ip>:8080/. Turn on the receiver's hotspot, join it from the laptop, and nothing touches the internet.
  • Even without a laptop: tap Open dashboard on this phone in the receiver.
  • This GitHub page is a demo and download page only. A secure (https) page cannot read from a phone's plain http address, so the real live view is the one the phone serves.

A triangle of 3 is a good minimum and a square of 4 is the sensible deployment. Details and caveats in the study.

Honest limits. The Android app has been compiled and its server and dashboard tested with a mock phone, but it has not yet been tried on real Bluetooth hardware by its author. Bluetooth RSSI is noisy (4–8 dB is normal), so expect 1–2 m typical error, not centimetres. A phone can only be a beacon if it supports BLE advertising (most Android phones from the last 6 years do). iPhones cannot run this app.
